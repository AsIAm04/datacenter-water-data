Where freshwater withdrawals are concentrated, by county

What this map is, and is not. It shows freshwater withdrawals reported or estimated by the U.S. Geological Survey for seven sectors, in million gallons per day (Mgal/d). It is not data center water use. Two optional layers sit on top: data center building footprint from OpenStreetMap, and site water use that Google and Microsoft have disclosed. Neither layer says what supplies a site, and the map does not model how much water a place has to spare.

Method notes and limits
  • Withdrawal, not consumption. USGS reports water withdrawn from ground and surface sources. It does not report how much is returned or evaporated, so withdrawal intensity here is not comparable to consumption figures reported by data center operators.
  • Freshwater only. Saline withdrawals are excluded. Values are groundwater plus surface water, fresh.
  • Seven sectors, harmonized across years: public supply, domestic self-supplied, irrigation (all irrigation types), thermoelectric power, industrial, mining, and livestock plus aquaculture. Thermoelectric is the sum of fossil, geothermal and nuclear categories in 1985 to 1995 and once-through plus closed-loop in 2000 to 2015. Livestock includes aquaculture from 1990 onward.
  • Commercial withdrawals are excluded. USGS reported commercial use only through 1995, so a consistent series does not exist. Data centers would sit partly in commercial and partly in public supply, so this baseline understates the sectors a data center draws on.
  • Double counting. Public supply deliveries serve homes, businesses and industry that also appear elsewhere in USGS tables; the seven sectors here are withdrawals at the source, not deliveries, so they add without overlap.
  • Thermoelectric dominates many county totals. Once-through power plant cooling can be larger than all other uses in a county combined. Use the checkbox to remove it.
  • Trends are approximate at county scale. Estimation methods and sector definitions changed between USGS compilations. National patterns are reliable; single-county jumps between years can reflect method changes or reporting differences.
  • Gray means no usable data. Counties missing from a year's compilation, or whose boundaries changed (old Alaska census areas, Yellowstone Park MT), are gray. Virgin Islands are not mapped. Independent Virginia cities merged into counties are summed into the county; Dade, Shannon and Wade Hampton are mapped to their renamed counties.
  • Series used. The USGS "Revised" county series, which differs slightly from the original 2015 release. The mapped 2015 national total is 280,436 Mgal/d versus 280,689 in the 2015 release (0.09% lower), mostly from revised irrigation values and unmapped territories.
  • 2020 and later. USGS changed to annual modeled estimates on HUC12 watersheds after 2015, so a 2020 county value is not directly comparable to this series and is not shown here.
  • HUC8 outlines are drawn over counties for reference only. Counties are the unit of measurement; watershed boundaries show where county values sit relative to hydrologic units.
  • Class breaks for volume measures are quantiles of nonzero county values pooled across all years, so colors mean the same thing in every year.
