# IM3 Open Source Data Center Atlas v2026.11.02

Source: Mongird, Vernon, Burleyson, Akdemir & Rice (2026), MSD-LIVE, https://doi.org/10.57931/17054299 (record qxpk8-yae09). Licence: ODbL 1.0; derived from OpenStreetMap contributors. Counties from Census 2024 boundaries.

Files: CSV (2,018 rows; point 128, building 1,636, campus 254) and GeoPackage (layers point, building, campus; WGS84).

Use notes (from the record): crowd-sourced and incomplete; campus and building polygons can overlap, so do not sum sqft across layers; facilities straddling a county line appear once per county with the same id; the atlas holds locations and footprint only, no water or power data. Footprint is a siting layer, not a water estimate.

## Google FY2024 disclosed water (separate layer, direct only)
google_fy2024_us_site_water.csv: 23 US sites from Google's 2025 environmental report (FY2024), million gallons withdrawal/discharge/consumption. Covers 79% of Google's reported 9,866 Mgal total withdrawal (rest is non-US and an unallocated "Other" line, not mapped). Operational water at the sites only; electricity-related (indirect) water is not included and must never be added. Site-to-county is by place name (inferred), cross-checked against the atlas for the Ohio sites and Lenoir NC; Midlothian TX not checked. New Albany OH straddles Franklin and Licking counties; its volume is not split and is compared with the two counties' combined supply. google_fy2024_share_of_local_supply.csv divides withdrawal and consumption (Mgal/yr / 366 days, 2024 leap year) by the county's mean local runoff (data/supply). Other operators are absent, so county values are a floor for all data centers.

## Update: New Albany OH and Microsoft FY25
- Google New Albany OH: CORRECTED. An earlier edit assigned it wholly to Licking County on a research agent's view that the Franklin overlap was a polygon artifact. User information shows the campus does span the Licking-Franklin line (Beech Rd SW parcels are Licking; property also at 5266 Babbitt Rd, Franklin), so records naming either county can describe different parcels of one development. It is treated as a straddling site: one Google figure, not split, compared with the two counties' combined supply. Water is supplied wholesale by Columbus Water & Power from sources in other counties. A 2025 utility-records figure (over 1 billion gallons, Scripps News) differs from Google's FY2024 405.3 Mgal and is not mixed in.
- microsoft_fy25_us_site_water.csv: 11 US locations, FY2025 (Jul 2024 to Jun 2025) withdrawal in megaliters from Table 15 of the 2026 Environmental Data Fact Sheet, converted to Mgal. Withdrawal only; Microsoft publishes no site-level consumption. Each ML value ties to the source's pool count (ML/2.5). Four locations are metros spanning several counties (Atlanta, Des Moines, San Antonio; Chicago mapped to Cook) and are compared with combined county supply, not split. Verified: all 11 US withdrawal values, and the 74% and 79% non-potable shares for Quincy and San Antonio, were re-read from the raw PDF table and match. Microsoft defines Location as a metropolitan area (city or regional cluster), so each county is inferred from the atlas, not stated by Microsoft.
- New Albany parcel check (2026-11-21), verified on the Franklin County Auditor site (property.franklincountyauditor.com, owner search): parcel 222-004984-00, owner MONTAUK INNOVATIONS LLC, 5266 Babbitt Rd, Plain Twp / New Albany corp., 222.086 acres, land use 375 Data Center, buildings 811,338 sq ft (built 2021 to 2024). Cards 1 and 2 show the same figure, so whether that is one building or two, and how the section rows relate to the building rows, is not resolved; no total is recorded (distinct building rows sum to 756,390 sq ft, or 898,378 if card 2 is separate). ## Google FY2025 and the 2024 to 2025 comparison (added 2026-11-21)
- google_fy2025_us_site_water.csv: 26 US rows from the Google 2026 Environmental Report, "Water use by data center location, 2025", p.97 (Jan 1 to Dec 31 2025). Withdrawal minus discharge equals consumption in every row (checked). All 26 rows matched Google's PDF in a text-extraction read on 2026-11-21.
- google_withdrawal_2024_vs_2025.csv: 23 paired sites (7,796.5 Mgal withdrawal in 2024, 10,659.0 in 2025). The map shows per site and does not present these sums as a headline. Ratios of very small values (Pflugerville, Phoenix, Wilmer) reflect Google's rounding of values under 50,000 gallons.
- Mesa AZ, New Haven IN and Red Oak TX appear in the 2025 table with no 2024 row. They are labeled "first appears in the 2025 table". Whether they are newly built, newly operating or newly reported is not established. Counties come from place names: Mesa is in Maricopa County and New Haven in Allen County (neither checked in the atlas, which lists Google only in Maricopa among the three), and Red Oak is in Ellis County (the atlas shows Google buildings in Ellis).
- FY2024 values were re-checked on 2026-11-21 against the 2025 report PDF (pages 110 to 111): every US row matches. Rows for Phoenix and Wilmer did not parse automatically and were read by eye.
- Douglas County GA: 2025 withdrawal 440.6, discharge 73.5, consumption 367.1 are consistent. The potable and reclaimed split is not shown for 2025, because extractions of page 97 disagree on which line is which (one read gave potable 46.3 and reclaimed 394.3, others the reverse). The 2024 split is verified in the PDF: potable 7.4, reclaimed wastewater 436.7.
- Footnote markers 229, 230, 233 and 235 (Pflugerville, Phoenix, Storey County, Wilmer) point to endnote 226, "Air-cooled facility", per a text read of the 2026 report on 2026-11-21. Endnote 224 says withdrawals are potable unless otherwise specified.
- Share-of-local-supply files (google_fy2024_share_of_local_supply.csv, google_fy2025_share_of_local_supply.csv) use 366 days for 2024 and 365 for 2025. Column names now say context ratio. The ratio is site withdrawal over local modeled runoff and is not a measure of the supply serving the site. The Microsoft table keeps its `withdrawal_pct_of_local_supply` column name and carries the same caveat.
- The map draws one diamond per site. Sites in the same county are fanned out around the county position, and New Albany is drawn once.
